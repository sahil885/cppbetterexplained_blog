# A Tour of C++ (3rd Edition) Review

**Short answer:** *A Tour of C++* is an excellent book for the reader it was written for: an experienced programmer who wants a fast, modern overview of C++. It isn't a beginner's book, and its author says so. If you've never programmed, start somewhere gentler and come back to it later. It'll make far more sense then.

## The Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Author | Bjarne Stroustrup, the creator of C++ |
| Publisher | Addison-Wesley |
| Edition | 3rd edition, September 2022 |
| Length | About 254 pages plus index (Stroustrup's figure) |
| C++ standard | C++20, plus a few features expected in C++23 |
| Written for | People who already know C++, or experienced programmers |

## What It Covers

The book is a quick tour of the whole of standard C++, both the language and the standard library, at what Stroustrup calls "a moderately high level". It has 19 chapters plus an appendix:

- **The language:** basics, user-defined types, modularity and separate compilation, error handling, classes, copy and move, templates, and C++20 concepts.
- **The standard library:** strings and regular expressions, input and output, containers, algorithms, ranges, pointers and containers, utilities, numerics, and concurrency, including coroutines.
- **Context:** a chapter on the history of C++, and an appendix on using the standard library as a module.

It's remarkable how much fits into about 250 pages. That's also the reason it's hard going for beginners.

## What It Does Well

**It's fast.** You see all of modern C++ in a few evenings, and you come away knowing what exists and where it fits.

**It's modern.** Concepts, ranges, modules and coroutines are all there. If your knowledge of C++ stops at C++11, this is the quickest way to catch up.

**It's from the source.** Stroustrup shows C++ the way it's meant to be used, and each chapter ends with practical advice.

**It shows how the pieces fit.** Instead of teaching features one at a time, it shows how the language and library work together.

## Why It's Hard for Beginners

**It assumes you can already program.** Stroustrup's own description says the book is for people "who already know C++ or at least are experienced programmers". That isn't modesty. The book is designed that way.

**It moves very quickly.** Chapter 1 covers programs, functions, types, scope, constants, pointers, arrays and references in under 20 pages. Pointers, arrays and references get about three of those pages. For someone who's programmed before, that's a useful refresher. For someone who hasn't, it's far too fast.

**It explains *what*, not *how to think*.** A beginner needs a lot of repetition and practice with the basics. A tour, by design, doesn't stop for long.

## Who Should Read It

- **Programmers coming from Python, Java, C# or JavaScript** who want to understand modern C++ quickly.
- **C++ programmers returning after a break**, or catching up with C++20.
- **Students after a first C++ course**, as a way to see the whole language at once.

## What Beginners Should Read Instead

If you're new to programming, Stroustrup himself points you to another of his books, *Programming: Principles and Practice Using C++*, which teaches programming from scratch. Your options:

- **[Programming: Principles and Practice Using C++](/posts/programming-principles-and-practice-review/)** — Stroustrup's book for people who have never programmed. Thorough, and 656 pages long.
- **[learncpp.com](https://www.learncpp.com/)** — free, starts from zero, and covers far more than a beginner needs.
- **C++ Better Explained** — a short, plain-English book with diagrams, focused on the parts beginners find hardest: pointers, memory and OOP. It's 87 pages, so you can finish it, then read *A Tour of C++* with the foundations in place.

## A Tour of C++ vs Stroustrup's Other Books

| | A Tour of C++ (3rd ed.) | Programming: Principles and Practice (3rd ed.) | The C++ Programming Language (4th ed.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Published | September 2022 | 2024 | May 2013 |
| Length | About 254 pages + index | 656 pages | 1,300+ pages |
| Written for | Experienced programmers | People who have never programmed | Programmers who want the complete language |
| C++ standard | C++20 | C++20 and C++23 | C++11 |

## The Verdict

For an experienced programmer, *A Tour of C++* is one of the best C++ books you can buy: short, modern and written by the person who designed the language. For a beginner it's the wrong first book. That's no criticism of the book; it simply wasn't written for you yet. Learn the fundamentals first, and keep it on your list for later.
