# C++ Primer vs C++ Primer Plus: Which Should You Buy?

**Short answer:** they're two completely different books that happen to share most of a name. **C++ Primer** (Lippman, Lajoie and Moo) is precise and thorough, and suits people who have programmed before. **C++ Primer Plus** (Stephen Prata) is gentler and more step-by-step, but it's 1,440 pages and its print edition is out of print. Both cover C++11.

## Why the Names Are So Confusing

The two books have different authors and were written independently. They're both published by Addison-Wesley, though, and both use "primer" in the old publishing sense: a thorough foundational text, not a quick start. It's easy to order one when you meant the other.

## Side by Side

| | C++ Primer | C++ Primer Plus |
|---|---|---|
| Authors | Stanley Lippman, Josée Lajoie, Barbara Moo | Stephen Prata |
| Current edition | 5th, 2012 | 6th, 2011 |
| Length | Just under 1,000 pages | 1,440 pages |
| C++ standard | C++11 | C++11 |
| Style | Precise and dense | Gradual, many short examples |
| Best for | People who have programmed before | Patient beginners |
| Availability | 5th edition available (a 6th is listed but not for sale) | Print edition out of print; eBook still sold |

## C++ Primer

**What it does well:** it's careful and exact. Definitions are right, concepts arrive in a sensible order, and it introduces `std::string` and `std::vector` before C-style arrays and raw pointers. Once you know C++, it stays useful as a reference for years.

**Where it's hard:** it moves quickly for someone who has never programmed, and it's close to 1,000 pages. A 6th edition has been listed by the publisher for a long time, but as of this writing it isn't for sale, so the 5th is the one to buy.

## C++ Primer Plus

**What it does well:** it teaches programming as well as C++. The publisher describes it as covering programming fundamentals, including structured code and top-down design, with many short examples that each show one or two ideas at a time. Every chapter ends with review questions and programming exercises, and there's a chapter on what C++11 added.

**Where it's hard:**

- **It's 1,440 pages.** That's a lot of book to finish on your own.
- **It teaches the C-style basics alongside C++.** For example, it covers character-array strings in the same chapter that introduces the `string` class. That's useful background, but it's not the modern-first order newer books use.
- **The print edition is out of print.** The publisher still sells the eBook, and you may find used print copies.

## Which Should You Choose?

- **You've programmed before, in any language:** choose **C++ Primer**. You'll skip material you already know and get a more precise explanation of C++ itself.
- **You've never programmed, you're patient, and you like lots of small examples and exercises:** choose **C++ Primer Plus**.
- **You've never programmed and want something modern:** neither. Look at *Programming: Principles and Practice Using C++*, which teaches from zero using C++20 and C++23 — see our [review](/posts/programming-principles-and-practice-review/).

## Are They Outdated?

Both describe C++11. Everything they teach is still valid C++, but neither covers what's been added since: nothing from C++14, C++17, C++20 or C++23. If you learn from either, plan to catch up later with something newer, such as the free [learncpp.com](/posts/is-learncpp-enough/), which includes an appendix on each standard from C++11 to C++23.

## If Neither Fits

- **[Programming: Principles and Practice Using C++](/posts/programming-principles-and-practice-review/)** — written for people who have never programmed, and the most up to date of the classic textbooks.
- **[learncpp.com](/posts/is-learncpp-enough/)** — free, starts from zero, and kept up to date.
- **C++ Better Explained** — short and plain-English, with diagrams, for true beginners. It focuses on the parts that stall most people: pointers, memory and OOP. It's 87 pages, so you can finish it first, then move on to a big textbook with the foundations in place.

## The Verdict

If you already program, **C++ Primer** is the better book. If you're starting from zero, **C++ Primer Plus** is the gentler of the two, but at 1,440 pages and from 2011, you may be better served by a newer or shorter book first. Either way, don't buy the wrong one by accident: check the authors on the cover.
